One page, one set of 20 sections. Resize the window or use the Width preview for 1920, 1440, 1024, 768, 390, 375 and 320 px. Premium editorial wireframe: colours, type and spacing come from the NUDGE design system; image areas are labelled placeholders, not photography. The NUDGE wordmark stands in for the logo. Visual direction: luxury beauty editorial

The page was recomposed section by section (20 sections in the revised workbook order): a campaign first fold (one full-width campaign image behind a transparent header, text bottom left, a featured-product card; v8.0, see below); a slim benefit strip; an asymmetric image-led category grid; circular concern tiles and numbered routine links; product cards with badges, a key-attribute line, shade dots where a product has shades, price and compare-at price, the whole card being the link (no Quick Add and no image rollover); a framed guided finder; a shop-the-look image with labelled hotspots and an overlapping product list; circular ingredient images; a how-to ritual journey (four staggered step cards on a warm band, swipeable on phones); a cinematic video band; an edge-to-edge before and after comparison with no text (After layered over Before, clipped at a draggable divider); one large review beside two smaller; a customer-photo mosaic; a compact recognition strip (one row of certification, award and press logos, each tagged and linked to its source); an overlapping promotion card; a newsletter on a warm band (heading left, form right, inline validation); a dark footer. Layouts are not reused from section to section. Original visual language; no layout, asset or branding was copied from other themes. Public theme demos (Prestige, Pipeline, Palo Alto, Motion, Sense) were not fetched during this work; the direction draws on general knowledge of how premium beauty themes present editorial layout, merchandising and shop-the-look.

Colours, type, spacing, radii and shadows come from the existing design tokens (ivory, blush, rose, charcoal, Cormorant headings, DM Sans UI). No token was changed and no colour was added. Image areas are soft tonal placeholders labelled with the subject and size; they are not photography and do not represent NUDGE products. Badges (Bestseller, 15% off, New), the compare-at price, the key-attribute lines and the shade dots are sample content.

Browser testing. Tested: Chrome and Edge on Windows (both Chromium, so they share one engine). Not tested: Firefox (not installed here), Safari and other WebKit browsers (not available on this machine), real phones and tablets. These browsers were reviewed by feature only, not run. The CSS and JavaScript use standard, widely supported features: Grid, Flexbox, custom properties, calc(), media queries, position: sticky with the -webkit- prefix, details/summary, and ES5 JavaScript. Aspect ratios use the padding technique rather than aspect-ratio, and no :has(), container queries, logical properties or inset are used. Items to watch in Firefox and Safari: flexbox gap needs Safari 14.1 or newer; spacing that used clamp() is now stepped through CSS custom properties at 600 and 1024 px (typography and spacing audit, 2026-11-11); clamp() remains only for a few viewport-proportional heights (hero stage, category rows); display: contents is used on the header utility group below 1024 px. Treat Firefox and Safari as untested until opened there.

Other limits. Checks are measurements, not a design review. Not tested: browser text zoom, landscape phones, screen readers. The drawer has no focus trap (a build task).

How to use: ritual journey (section 11)

Revised after the interactive version (one large image stage beside a text list) read as empty and list-like. Now a ritual journey on a warm blush band: four portrait step cards in a row, steps 2 and 4 set lower so the row reads as a sequence. Each card has a 4:5 application image, a large italic rose numeral overlapping the image's lower edge, the step title, a one-line instruction, tip and time, and the product link. Everything is visible at once; no interaction is needed on desktop or tablet. Phones get a swipeable row (78% cards, the next one peeking) with a "01 / 04" counter and 44 px arrows, which is shorter and easier to browse than a long vertical list.

  • Layout: from 1024 px four cards in a row (the even ones 72 px lower); below 1024 px the swipe row (44% cards from 600 px, 78% below). A 2 by 2 tablet grid was tried and dropped: it made the section 1,706 px tall at 768.
  • Accessibility: an ordered list; each title is a level 3 heading with a hidden "Step n." prefix; the swipe row is a focusable list (arrow keys scroll it); the counter is a polite live region; the arrows are named and disable at the ends.
  • Content: step names follow the routine on this page and link to the same products; instructions, tips, times and the closing tip are placeholders to be taken from the product labels. Nothing is invented.

Before & After: edge-to-edge comparison slider (section 13)

One compact frame spanning the full page width with no heading, copy or padding; the only text is the BEFORE and AFTER labels inside the frame. The Before image is the base layer; the After image sits on top with exactly the same box and is clipped with clip-path: inset(0 0 0 var(--pos)), so the left of the divider shows Before and the right shows After. The divider starts at 50%. Frame ratio: 1:1 below 600 px (390 x 390), 16:9 from 600 px (768 x 432), 21:9 from 1024 px capped at 76% of the screen height (1440 x 609, 1920 x 816), so the whole section fits in one screen. BEFORE is placed bottom left inside the Before layer and AFTER bottom right inside the After layer, so each travels with its image; a label fades out once the divider reaches it rather than being cut in half. The placeholders: Before is flat blush, After is hatched rose; their names stay in the markup, visually hidden.

  • Input: a native range input (0 to 100, visually hidden, keyboard and screen-reader operable; aria-valuetext reads "40% before, 60% after"); pointer events on the frame for mouse, pen and touch; touch-action: pan-y keeps vertical page scrolling. The focus ring shows on the round handle.
  • Removed at the merchant's request: heading, description, product and duration details, hint, the BEFORE and AFTER labels, and the results disclaimer. The workbook still flags this section for claim-safe images and legal review; if a disclaimer is needed, add it in a neighbouring section or the image itself.

Shopify build and Tailwind

Each of the 20 sections is one Shopify section file in sections/ (announcement bar, header, hero, benefits, category list, featured collection, concern list, finder, shop the look, ingredients, how to use, video, before and after, testimonials, photo gallery, logo list, promotional banner, custom liquid, newsletter, footer). They are placed in templates/index.json in this order; the header, announcement bar and footer are section groups. Repeating items are blocks with settings (category tile, product tab, pin, ingredient, ritual step, quote, photo, logo, benefit); section settings carry the eyebrow, heading, text, colour scheme and spacing. Shared pieces are snippets: product card, button, image placeholder or image with focal point, section heading, text link, icon. The product cards read the native product, collection and variant objects, the newsletter is Shopify’s customer form, and the shop-the-look pins are blocks. Tailwind utilities compile (Tailwind CLI, same token-mapped config) into one theme stylesheet in assets/; the in-browser Tailwind script is for the wireframes only. No app is involved.
